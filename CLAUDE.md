# Wrenn's Roost — Prose Style Rules

Standing rules for writing chapters of the "Wrenn's Roost" serialized story. Apply these to every chapter before it's shown to the user, not just when a specific line gets flagged.

## Punctuation

- Never use em dashes (—) or en dashes (–). Use periods, commas, or restructure the sentence instead.

## Dialogue

- Characters should speak often, with natural, warm greetings ("Good evening, Dranzer!") rather than being described speaking.
- Dialogue should carry plot and character information wherever possible instead of narration doing it for them.

## Physical description

- Be descriptive and concrete about how characters physically do their work and move through space (hands, tools, posture, the actual mechanics of an action) — not just that they did something.
- Avoid vague placeholder description that sounds evocative but conveys nothing concrete. Reaching for "something happened, quick and [adjective]" instead of describing the actual physical detail is a failure mode, not a style. If a moment calls for a character's reaction, name the actual physical thing that happened (a muscle in the jaw, fingers tightening on a chair arm, eyebrows lifting) rather than gesturing at "something" crossing/flickering/shifting.

## Closing-statement rule (added after repeated correction — see below for why this exists)

**Never end a paragraph, scene, or chapter on a tag clause that restates a beat the action/dialogue already conveyed.** This includes constructions like:

- `..., since [explanation of why the preceding thing happened or what it meant].`
- `..., the way he'd learned to [abstract lesson], and [continued sentence].`
- `..., which was [interpretive label for what just happened].`

These constructions sound reflective but are almost always dead weight: if the scene already showed the beat, restating it in an appended clause doesn't add meaning, it just softens the ending and pads the sentence. When a sentence like this shows up, especially at the end of a paragraph, scene break, or chapter, cut the tag clause and let the sentence end at the concrete action.

**Bad:** `Dorneth nodded, small and unconvinced, and went back to the Veinsilver, and Dranzer let him have the silence, since some things a person needed to sit with alone before anyone else's reassurance could actually reach them.`

**Fixed:** `Dorneth nodded, small and unconvinced, and went back to the Veinsilver, and Dranzer let him have the silence.`

**Bad:** `He let it go, for now, the way he'd learned to let things go that weren't done cooking, and rode the rest of the way home with Dorneth's straighter shoulders as the one thing about the whole ordeal he was willing to call, plainly and without qualification, a win.`

**Fixed:** `He let it go, for now, and rode the rest of the way home with Dorneth's straighter shoulders as the one thing about the whole ordeal he was willing to call a win.`

### Why this rule exists

This got flagged and fixed as a one-off line edit four separate times (Chapters 5, 9, 10, and 11) before it was written down as a standing rule. Each time, only the specific flagged sentence got corrected — the fix never generalized into something actively checked on the next chapter, so the same reflex resurfaced at the next scene or chapter ending, which is exactly where this habit fires hardest (it's a "landing" reflex for closing a beat, not a random word choice). Writing it down here is meant to break that cycle: check every paragraph/scene/chapter ending against this rule before publishing, don't wait for it to be flagged again.

## Continuity discipline

- Before writing a scene, check established canon (character ages, prior visits to a location, existing relationships) rather than assuming a "first encounter" framing by default. E.g., a character who has visited a place before shouldn't react to it with first-time awe.
- When new details are established in prose (physical descriptions, new named characters, plot developments), sync them back to the Trello world-bible after a batch of chapters, not necessarily after every single chapter.

## Chapter format

- Longer, novel-style chapters.
- POV can rotate to whichever character a scene genuinely belongs to.
- Give an outline before writing a chapter when starting a new major arc beat, a new POV, or a new setting reveal — not required for every chapter once a scene type is already established.
